# Forever

Two addons for **WoW Forever** (interface 16001). Each stands on its own and is turned on or off
in the game's AddOns list.

| Addon               | What it is                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Forever Gearbox** | Small quality-of-life tweaks, an addon of its own with its own page in Settings > AddOns (in Forever++'s look), with or without [Forever++](https://github.com/xIGBClutchIx/ForeverPlusPlus); its Forever++ Integration (off by default) works together with Forever++. `/fg` |
| **Forever Codex**   | A journal of the creatures you meet, the quests and items you find, where they are on the map, and badges, learned as you play (and importable from Forever Bestiary). It has its own settings window, minimap button and Update Notice. `/codex`                             |

Planned: a recipe book and a pet journal, each its own addon.

Both carry the same small runtime (`Shared/`: events, modules, saved variables, slash commands,
Edit Mode, popups and windows), each a copy in its own folder, so neither needs the other. Until
3.0.0 they were a suite with a third addon, Forever Core; it's gone now, and each takes over what
it kept for it.

## Install

Copy the `ForeverGearbox` and `ForeverCodex` folders into
`World of Warcraft\_forever_\Interface\AddOns\` (Forever Gearbox works on its own; Forever++ is optional) and restart
the game. Remove an old `ForeverCore` folder: nothing needs it. Settings from earlier versions come
over once: Forever Gearbox' into its own (from Forever++'s profile too, where 3.0.0 to 3.2.0 kept them),
Forever Core's (the settings window's place, chat, the minimap button, the Update Notice) into
Forever Codex. Once the GitHub repository is public, an addon manager such as WowUp can
install both from the repository's URL and update them from its releases.

Before 3.3.0, they were known as Forever Tweaks (`ForeverTweaks`) and Forever Compendium (`ForeverCompendium`).
The game keeps an addon's saved data in a file named after its folder, so to keep yours, close the
game first and, in `WTF\Account\<account>\SavedVariables\` and each character's `SavedVariables\`
folder, copy `ForeverTweaks.lua` to `ForeverGearbox.lua` and `ForeverCompendium.lua` to
`ForeverCodex.lua`; then delete the old addon folders. At the next login each addon takes its old
saved variables over (`formerSaved` in its `Part.lua`), and Forever++ moves each module's settings
to its new name by itself.

Forever Gearbox keeps what its modules collect (lists, sessions) in `ForeverGearboxDB` and
`ForeverGearboxCharDB`; Forever Codex everything in `ForeverCodexDB` and
`ForeverCodexCharDB`.

For development, `scripts/link.sh "<game>/Interface/AddOns"` links both folders from this
repository into the game, replacing old links of the same name; remove the old `ForeverCore` link
yourself.

## Forever Gearbox

Small quality-of-life tweaks, an addon of its own: its modules are on its own page in Settings >
AddOns > Forever Gearbox (`/fg`), drawn just as Forever++'s Modules page is (the module list by
category with a search box and checkboxes, the chosen module's options beside it, a module's own
list such as Auto Buy's under its options), with Advanced Graphics under it while it's on, and no
window of its own. Its settings are in `ForeverGearboxDB`, Forever++ installed or not. Forever
Gearbox complements Forever++: a feature both have may be on twice, and you turn one off yourself.

**Forever++ Integration** (a module under Tweaks for Addons, offered while Forever++ is loaded, off
by default) works together with Forever++; off, Forever Gearbox leaves Forever++ alone:

- Upgrade Arrows in Baganator judges gear by Forever++'s Upgrade Arrows instead of its own.
- Quest Markers, Fishing and Upgrade Arrows in Baganator are also a "Forever Gearbox" section on the
  page of the Forever++ module they go with (Quest Nameplates, Fishing Cast, Upgrade Arrows; after
  a /reload), and Quest Markers hides Forever++'s own nameplate mark where it draws one.
- Advanced Graphics is listed under Forever++ in Settings too.
- A console variable a module asks for gets a button finding it in Forever++'s Console Variables,
  and what modules ask of Forever++ is answered (Fade Chat Messages leaves messages to Keep Chat
  Visible while that's on).

Gray notices say when another addon or a game setting needs changing for a module to work its
best; a console variable a module needs is asked for the way Plumber asks.

### Modules

Where a module does something WilduTools also does (Five-Second Rule, Cast Bar), it leaves the feature to them while theirs is on,
and its page says so. Forever Gearbox may also do what one of Forever++'s modules does: with both
addons on, turn off the one you don't want (nothing says so). Nothing is only a console variable,
which Forever++'s Console Variables sets and locks (as `rotateMinimap`). Module text is written the way Forever++'s is.

A module that needs QuestieDB, or another addon that isn't there, is red and locked off without
it (a padlock for its checkbox), its page showing why.

**FPS & Latency and Layer Info** start just above the quest tracker, under the minimap. FPS & Latency sits left of center, Layer Info right of it, and both follow the tracker when you move it.

- **Move them in Edit Mode** (Esc → Edit Mode). Drag one to move it; it snaps to the screen's center and edges. Click it for a dialog with a size slider, and use the arrow keys to nudge it. Right-click it for Size and Reset Position.
- **Or use the options:** Horizontal Position, Vertical Position, Text Size and Outline.
- Outside Edit Mode they can't be dragged, so they never move by accident. Right-click one for its menu.

### Commands

| Command                             | Does                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `/fg`                               | Opens Forever Gearbox' page in Settings.                                                                        |
| `/fg <module>`                      | Opens them on that module, for example `/fg full bags`.                                                         |
| `/fg list`                          | Lists the modules and whether each is on.                                                                       |
| `/fg options <module>`              | Lists a module's options and their values.                                                                      |
| `/fg toggle <module> [option]`      | Turns a module, or one of its checkboxes, on or off.                                                            |
| `/fg set <module> <option> <value>` | Changes an option: `on`/`off`, a number, or a choice by name. For example, `/fg set performance latency local`. |

| `/fg help`                          | Lists the commands.                                                                                             |

`/forevergearbox` works too. Modules and options can be named by the start of their name or title, without spaces. All of these work in macros.

### For other addons

`ForeverGearboxAPI.v1` (also `ForeverTweaksAPI.v1`, its name before 3.3.0) is read-only. It returns copies, and an error inside it returns nil rather than breaking your code. It covers Forever Gearbox' modules (from 2.0.0 to 2.9.0 it covered every Forever addon's); each row of `GetModules()` and each module callback still names the addon. An error in your callback is logged, not reported as Forever Gearbox'. `API.lua` documents every call:

```lua
local api = ForeverGearboxAPI and ForeverGearboxAPI.v1
if api then
    local fps, localMs, worldMs = api.GetPerformance()
    api.RegisterCallback("LAYER_CHANGED", function(event, zoneUID, nwbLayer) end)
end
```

The callbacks are `READY`, `MODULE_TOGGLED`, `OPTION_CHANGED` and `LAYER_CHANGED`. `v1` only grows: nothing in it is renamed or removed. `GetErrorCount` is kept but always returns nil since the Error Catcher was removed in 1.6.0.

## Forever Codex

Its book of creatures, NPCs and items is learned as you play: no database is shipped. It needs
Forever Gearbox; with QuestieDB installed, its Quests page lists every quest your
character can take and what they ask, and its links reach what you haven't met yet. It never
touches the quest tracker and says nothing in chat; its sounds (the journal's, notifications',
badges') go off with one checkbox (Journal > Sounds). Open the
journal with `/codex` or a click on its minimap button; right-click the button (or `/codex settings`) for
its settings window. It does what Forever Bestiary did, from your own play.

## Add a module

1. Create `<Addon>/Modules/<Category>/MyFeature.lua` in the Forever addon it belongs to (its category is one of that addon's `categories` in its `Part.lua`; each folder under `Modules/` is one category). Code several modules share, that isn't a module itself, goes in the addon's `Lib/`; a module's own helper file sits beside it, headed with its path.
2. Add it to that addon's `.toc` (in Forever Gearbox, above `API.lua`).
3. Put its text in that addon's `Locales/enUS_Modules.lua`.
4. Add a line to `ForeverCodex/Changelog.lua` (both addons' releases), and set `added = "<version>"` so it gets a NEW label.
5. Run `lua5.1 tests/run.lua`, then restart the game. A new file needs a restart; after edits, `/reload` is enough.

A module:

```lua
local M = ns:NewModule("MyFeature", {
    title = L.MY_TITLE,
    category = "interface",           -- one of the addon's categories (Part.lua)
    group = L.GROUP_COMBAT,            -- optional: related modules of a category sit together under
                                      -- this small heading (Fishing, Combat, Nameplates)
    description = L.MY_DESC,
    added = "1.1.0",
    defaults = { enabled = true, size = 12, mode = "simple", history = {} },
    perCharacter = { history = true }, -- this table is kept per character
    options = {
        { key = "size", type = "slider", label = L.MY_SIZE, min = 8, max = 24, step = 1 },
        { key = "mode", type = "dropdown", label = L.MY_MODE, section = L.MY_LOOK,
          choices = { { "simple", L.MY_SIMPLE }, { "detailed", L.MY_DETAILED } } },
        ns.ChatOption(),              -- a Chat Messages checkbox, for a module that talks unprompted
    },
    notice = { text = L.MY_NOTICE, button = L.TURN_ON, shown = function(db) return not db.size end,
               fn = function() end },  -- shown(db) gets the module's settings, like option.shown
    -- features = { "a", "b" },      -- checkbox option keys that are each a feature: then the module
                                      -- has no on/off checkbox and runs while any is ticked
})

local function onTarget(self, event) self:Print("size", self.db.size) end

function M:OnEnable()  self:RegisterEvent("PLAYER_TARGET_CHANGED", onTarget) end
function M:OnDisable() end            -- its events, timers and hooks stop by themselves
function M:OnSettingChanged(key, value) end
function M:DebugLine() return "size " .. self.db.size end  -- optional: its line in the debug report
```

A Forever Gearbox module that goes with one of Forever++'s own sets `attach = "<Forever++ module>"` (`Core/Attach.lua`): with Forever++ Integration on it's also a "Forever Gearbox" section on that module's page in Forever++'s Settings (`UI/ForeverPage.lua`); it stays a module of its own either way.

Option fields:

- `key` is the saved setting; its default goes in `defaults`.
- `type` is `checkbox`, `slider` (`min`, `max`, `step`, `format`) or `dropdown` (`choices`). `input = true` adds a box to type an exact value in (for wide ranges where the number matters); `unit` sets the text after it (taken from `format` otherwise) and `precision` the decimals a typed value keeps (the step's by default).
- `label` and `tooltip` are the text; `section` starts a header.
- `requires = "key"` indents the option under a checkbox and greys it out while that checkbox is off.
- `shown = function(db)` hides the option when it returns false.
- `group = { label, format, tooltip }` on checkboxes: every checkbox with the same group table is drawn as one row, a dropdown that ticks each, its button counting the ticked ones with `format` ("%d hidden"). Each stays its own setting, for `/fg set` and Defaults.
- `slider = "key"` puts a slider on the same row as this checkbox.
- `conflicts = { { key = "other", when = function(db) end, text = L.WHY } }` shows a warning while `when` is true, with a button that jumps to the other option.
- `added = "<version>"` gives the option a NEW label.

Rules that keep the addon light:

- **Events, timers and hooks belong to their module.** Use `self:RegisterEvent(event, fn)` (calling it again with the same function, or `self:UnregisterEvent` for an event it doesn't hear, costs nothing, so register and unregister freely as an option changes), `self:After` and `self:NewTicker` for timers (`self:Soon(fn)` to redo something once on the next frame after a burst of events, `self:IsPending(timer)` and `self:CancelTimer(timer)` to check or stop one), `self:Hook` and `self:HookScript` for hooks (`self:HookCallback(target, key, install, fn)` for a tooltip post call or callback registry), `self:OnNews(fn)` and `self:OnChanged(fn)` for the suite's news (`ns:Announce`) and setting changes. They all run through one shared frame with `securecallfunction`, so an error in one never stops the others. They stop or go quiet when the module turns off, and the Profiler times them. A console variable a module needs is a notice instead: `notice = { requires = { { cvar = ..., value = ... } } }` asks the player to set it.
- **A module that's off costs nothing.** Make frames in `OnEnable`, not when the file loads.
- **Settings and data are kept apart.** `self.db` gives the player's setting, else the default. A table default is data the module keeps, and Defaults never clears it. When the saved layout changes, add a step in the addon's migrations file (`ForeverGearbox/Migrations.lua`, `ForeverCodex/Migrations.lua`): `ns:Migrate(version, fn)` for the account's file (bump `layout` in its `Part.lua`), `ns:MigrateChar(version, fn)` for the character's (bump `charLayout`).
- **Talk in chat only when the player asked.** Otherwise use `self:Debug`, which goes to the log, or `self:Print` with `ns.ChatOption()`, which gives the player a Chat Messages checkbox.
- **Ask with `ns:Confirm(M, key, text, onAccept)` and `ns:Prompt(M, key, text, onAccept)` (and show text to copy with `ns:ShowCopyText(key, label, text)`),** not your own windows. The answer runs as the module's code.
- **Don't open Blizzard windows from addon code, and wrap anything that might be protected in `ns:AfterCombat(fn)`.** The one exception is the bags (Full Bags' Open Bags), and only out of combat.
- **Only use APIs, templates and atlases that Forever has.** See `docs/forever-api.md`, and add anything new you check.
- **For something on screen, use `ns.Widgets.AddDisplay(M, { name, onEnter, menu })`** (`menu(add)` calls `add(text, fn)` per right-click entry) with `defaults = ns.Widgets.DisplayDefaults({ ... }, anchor)` (the anchor is given there, once) and `options = ns.Widgets.DisplayOptions({ ... })`. It shows and hides with the module and gives you Edit Mode, resizing and the position sliders for free; the module only sets its text with `display:SetLine(text)` (`display:GetLine()` reads it back).
- **New commands:** a module adds its own with `self:AddCommand(name, usage, description, fn)` (only offered while it's on; `fn(rest)` runs as the module's code); core commands use `ns:AddCommand`.

## Add a Forever addon

1. Make its folder with `<Name>.toc` (`## Version:` the others'), the runtime's files first
   (`Shared\...`, as the others list them), then a `Part.lua` calling `ns:NewMember(name, ns, { title,
short, saved, charSaved, slash, categories })` (see `Shared/Core/Members.lua`), its saved
   variables in the TOC, and an `Init.lua` last (Forever Codex's, or Forever Gearbox' for a
   Forever++ companion).
2. Its category labels (`L.CATEGORY_<KEY>`) in its `Locales/enUS.lua`.
3. Lay it out like the others: `Migrations.lua` at its root, `Lib/` for libraries several of its modules share, `UI/` for shared on-screen pieces, one `Modules/<Category>/` folder per category, and every file headed `<Addon> - <module title>` (a module's file) or `<Addon> - <its path>` (any other); the `lint` scenario checks the headers.
4. Add the folder to `scripts/link.sh`, `scripts/sync-shared.sh`, the two workflows, the `suite` scenario and the `lint` scenario's list, and its `Shared/` to `.desloppify/config.json`'s excludes.

## Releasing

1. Bump `## Version` in both addons' `.toc` (they share one version) and add the release to `ForeverCodex/Changelog.lua`.
2. Run `scripts/sync-shared.sh` if `Shared/` changed, then push a tag `v<version>` (`git tag v3.0.0 && git push origin v3.0.0`).

`.github/workflows/release.yml` then runs the tests, checks the tag matches every TOC, and publishes a GitHub release with `Forever-<version>.zip` (both folders), a `release.json` for addon managers (WowUp reads it), and that version's changelog as its notes. It also uploads the zip to CurseForge, filed under the game version named by the `CF_GAME_VERSION` repository variable (else the newest Retail one), once two things are set up: `## X-Curse-Project-ID: <id>` in `ForeverCodex.toc` and a `CF_API_KEY` repository secret (a token from https://legacy.curseforge.com/account/api-tokens). Until then that step is skipped.

## Tests

`lua5.1 tests/run.lua` loads the addons from their TOCs into a small stand-in for the game (`tests/wow.lua`; `W.LoadAddon` loads an addon's dependencies first: Forever Gearbox brings the Forever++ stand-in, `tests/stubs/foreverplusplus.lua`, which checks what's registered as Forever++ does and has its Modules page as `W.FPP`) and runs each scenario (a file in `tests/scenarios/core/`, `tweaks/`, `interface/`, `quests/`, `bags/` or `compendium/`) in its own Lua process; `lua5.1 tests/run.lua <name>` runs one. A scenario file must be listed in `tests/run.lua`'s `GROUPS` (the run fails otherwise). `W.RunTimers()` runs every pending timer; `W.Advance(seconds)` moves the clock and runs timers as they fall due:

- `suite`: both addons side by side: a namespace and saved variables each, no Forever Core, each slash speaking for its own addon, Gearbox' modules on Forever++'s page, Codex's minimap button and settings, the member API;
- `alone`: Forever Codex with only what it needs (Forever Gearbox, which brings Forever++), and Forever Core's saved settings brought over;
- `migrate`: Gearbox 1.10.0's and Codex 0.3.0's saved variables carried to today's layout (Gearbox' settings and on/off its own; Codex's friendly NPCs split from its creatures);
- `upgrade`: Gearbox 0.9.0's saved variables moved to today's layout;

GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/check.yml`) runs it on every push, along with a syntax check, an ASCII check, a check that each addon's TOC lists every one of its files, that each addon's `Shared/` is the repository's, and that every TOC has the same version. The game is still the real test: the stand-in checks wiring and logic, not looks.
